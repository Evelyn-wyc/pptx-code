# Research presentation workflow

Read for research argument design, evidence selection, or body positioning. Shared typography, notes, implementation, and delivery requirements are in [SKILL.md](../SKILL.md).

## Argument and page map

Start with the audience's questions: why the problem matters; the gap/constraint; the research path and its logical steps; the evidence; reliability limits; and remaining work if the study is open. A completed study ends with its selected method, evidence, and boundary; future work is optional.

Inventory each experiment/activity as:

`question or phenomenon → reason and controlled comparison → evidence → supported interpretation → next question`

Review before construction. A page should resolve a preceding uncertainty or establish an independent premise. Distinguish exploration, hypothesis tests, and retrospective mechanism studies; retain mixed/negative findings when they change the inference.

Use one page-map row per slide:

| Question/job | Reason and comparison | Topic title | Internal takeaway | Primary visual | Speaking purpose and next connection | Source/basis |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

The takeaway guides the title, visual, and speech; it is not another text box. Common jobs are problem, motivation, method/rule, result, explanation, and boundary. Split pages when jobs need separate evidence or conclusions. Keep titles short, meaningful, and consistent; approved covers, agendas, and section openers may differ.

The evidence ledger records source, as-of date/cutoff, sample/eligibility, period, benchmark, metric/weighting definitions, and fitted/validation/replay status. Check these before grouping results to prevent incompatible comparisons and hindsight leakage. Keep qualitative classification evidence with original text, date, and decision rule; use primary/authoritative sources for policy, industry, and company facts.

## Choose evidence before prose

| Analytical job | Useful visual |
|---|---|
| Time path | Line or area chart |
| Group/method comparison | Bar, dot, slope, compact table |
| Distribution | Histogram, box/violin plot, percentile bands |
| Composition | Stacked bar, matrix, restrained share chart |
| Decision sequence | Flow or funnel |
| Exact multi-metric results | Table |
| Source-backed cases | Table with date, original evidence, rule |
| A few short judgments | Text cards |

Use a dominant visual or readable related panels answering one question. Reuse original figures/PPT/HTML assets; crop/enlarge suitable panels before reconstructing them. Conceptual diagrams may be native shapes/SVGs. Decorative imagery must not imply evidence or displace plots. Keep brief labels, necessary annotations, and citations visible; explain the reasoning in notes. Omit decorative card-side strips and routine bottom explanation/configuration lines, following SKILL.md.

Organize related work around specific, fair unresolved obstacles. Show the framework before modules, then explain each module's inputs, operations, and outputs. Use a worked example or diagram before expanding an abstract formal argument. Place retrospective studies where they explain the observed finding.

Explain a method's choice, purpose, rule, failure conditions, and domain meaning. State whether mixed evidence supports selection, classification, explanation, monitoring, or no current use; preserve uncertainty, sample limits, and concentrated results. Observation alone is not proof.

## Speech and timing

A useful spoken order is preceding idea → visual encodings → question/comparison in each panel → interpretation/qualification → next unresolved question. End naturally at the conclusion. Explain substantive visuals in reading order, including reasons for design choices, without inventing unseen experiments or reciting every value. A table may need silent reading time: state the pattern, then discuss one or two representative entries.

Allocate time by novelty and importance: move quickly through background/standard procedures; spend time on original methods, decisive/surprising results, and boundaries. Estimate/rehearse with figure pauses. Reduce secondary content or merge overlapping pages when long; do not shrink type, rush speech, or leave visuals unexplained.

## Body-region layout

1. **Locate the content rectangle.** Use actual master/slide geometry and intended clearance: below the separator or approved header, above the footer or necessary source block, inside template side margins. Title, separator, footer, and page number remain outside it.
2. **Measure visible content.** Treat charts with axes/ticks/legends/annotations, tables, cards, captions, and body copy as one composition. Ignore hidden objects, transparent extents, background-only shapes, and unused image padding. Crop unnecessary asset whitespace or rebuild a plot before compensating with offsets.
3. **Balance and scale.** Balance outer gaps and optical weight while preserving reading order, chart-label relationships, columns, and internal left alignment. Move related objects together. Intentional asymmetry may be appropriate; centering does not require centered paragraphs. Enlarge undersized evidence and reduce redundant callouts. A wide bottom banner or decorative frame cannot hide a crowded upper visual or satisfy centering by itself.
4. **Check the rendered result.** Inspect all four outer gaps, plot area, internal spacing, labels/legends, limits, aspect ratio, and text-to-frame padding at presentation scale. Geometry/pixel bounds are diagnostics. Rerender corrections and record material intentional asymmetry in project records.

For generated charts placed directly on a slide, match figure, axes, and exported backgrounds exactly; use a deliberate coordinated tint when separation is useful. Avoid accidental opaque-white rectangles; verify transparency and SVG behavior in the target application.

## Review

Apply SKILL.md's font, contrast, reserve, and padding requirements to native objects and imported figures. Inspect rendered pages, pair each with its speech, and preserve the latest approved/manual version for later revisions. Remove redundant takeaways that compete with the primary evidence.

Single-slide test:

> one clear question + sufficient visual evidence + a supported conclusion + a complete spoken explanation

This is a review criterion, not a layout of four text boxes.
